Production deployments across two countries

Enterprise Traceability & Material Movement

Business context

Fresh produce changes location, condition, and unit of measure as it moves from a farm through import, storage, ripening, and packing. Quality records often sit outside the inventory workflow.

The enterprise needed a connected view of material, stock, and inspection evidence across both origin and destination operations.

Objective

Make every packed box traceable to its source and inspection history, while keeping quantities reconciled across the chain.

My responsibility

  • Owned the platform roadmap and solution design for enterprise traceability and material movement.
  • Translated field operations into the chain-and-node model, unit conversions, inventory states, and traceability requirements.
  • Coordinated product, engineering, and customer operations through implementation and rollout.

Operating workflow

  1. Register the source lot and attach its origin inspection and quality result.
  2. Record dispatch and inbound receipt against the same material identity.
  3. Convert containers into pallets while preserving quantities and parent-child links.
  4. Track partial movement, storage, and ripening without losing the remaining balance.
  5. Create packed boxes linked to the input lots and subsequent dispatch.
  6. Retrieve the source, handoffs, and quality history from an individual box.

Key decisions

  • Use configurable nodes so different supply chains share one platform foundation.
  • Require confirmation at material handoffs and unit conversions.
  • Support split inventory and parallel operating states.
  • Make reconciliation and reverse traceability core requirements for rollout.

Technology and controls

A shared lot and movement model connects inventory balances, conversion rules, inspection evidence, and reconciliation checks. Queries follow parent-child relationships from a box back to its source.

User experience

  • Field and node mobile app
  • Inventory dashboard
  • Movement confirmations
  • Reverse traceability view
  • Management reporting

Delivery scope

  • Production chain-and-node workflows across two countries
  • Live inventory by node and lot, including split states
  • Box-level source and quality traceability
  • Reconciliation alerts and connected inspection evidence

Business outcomes

Operations teams can locate stock, investigate quantity differences, and retrieve the origin and quality history of a packed box from the same record. The platform establishes a reusable foundation for enterprise traceability across different commodities.

Leadership contribution

Combined platform strategy, detailed operating-model design, and enterprise implementation. The key responsibility was making a shared data model work across teams, locations, and physical material transformations.

National Food Surveillance & Lab Compliance

Business context

Food surveillance spans field sampling, transport, laboratory testing, and regulatory review. Paper records and disconnected status updates made it difficult to locate samples and coordinate decisions.

Long surveillance cycles delayed the use of test results in compliance action.

Objective

Give officers current, traceable evidence from sample collection through laboratory results and compliance review.

My responsibility

  • Led product definition and workflow digitization across field, laboratory, and regulatory users.
  • Connected sample identity, chain of custody, test results, and compliance reporting.
  • Led development of the RAG-powered compliance capability and its review workflow.

Operating workflow

  1. Register the purchased sample with photographs, location, time, and seal details.
  2. Track collection and transport to the assigned accredited laboratory.
  3. Capture results against the sample, commodity, and relevant test parameters.
  4. Retrieve the applicable regulatory requirements and flag evidence gaps.
  5. Route the assessment to an authorized officer and update program dashboards.

Key decisions

  • Design the sampler app and compliance portal around one sample record.
  • Preserve traceable evidence through each custody handoff.
  • Use retrieval to connect regulatory explanations to source material.
  • Separate model-assisted interpretation from rule checks and authorized decisions.

Technology and controls

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) supports regulatory interpretation using relevant standards and laboratory evidence. Rules identify parameter exceptions, while officers retain responsibility for compliance decisions.

User experience

  • Sampler mobile app
  • Compliance portal
  • Laboratory status tracking
  • Program dashboards
  • Geographic sample view

Delivery scope

  • Digital sampling, custody tracking, and laboratory coordination
  • Spice surveillance spanning 10+ commodities and 50+ accredited laboratories
  • Fruit and vegetable surveillance across 20+ commodities
  • Centralized compliance reporting and regulatory review

Business outcomes

Teams gained current sample status and linked test evidence in one workflow. Officers could follow each sample from collection through laboratory analysis and regulatory review, with clear visibility into pending steps.

Leadership contribution

Led a program across government stakeholders, laboratories, logistics, and technology teams. The work combined institutional stakeholder management, workflow redesign, and careful use of AI in decision support.

Smart Kitchen & Food Waste Intelligence

Business context

Large catering operations had limited data on what was wasted, when it happened, and why. Manual logging made it difficult to separate overproduction, preparation loss, and plate waste.

Paper temperature records and disconnected audit data also limited visibility across kitchen operations.

Objective

Give kitchen managers reliable daily evidence to reduce waste and monitor operating conditions without slowing service.

My responsibility

  • Led solution definition and deployment across kitchen workflows, connected scales, sensors, and analytics.
  • Prioritized capture simplicity for staff and useful decision views for managers.
  • Connected food waste measurement with the wider kitchen quality and compliance program.

Operating workflow

  1. Capture waste weight using a connected scale.
  2. Capture an image and suggest the waste category or dish.
  3. Allow staff to confirm or correct the classification.
  4. Collect temperature and humidity readings from connected sensors.
  5. Review waste by dish, service period, location, and reason, alongside condition alerts.

Key decisions

  • Keep waste capture short enough to fit service operations.
  • Use a multimodal API to establish the classification workflow before committing to custom model development.
  • Retain staff corrections as useful labeled data.
  • Connect waste, sensors, audits, and operating procedures in a shared management view.

Technology and controls

Connected weighing scales and image-based classification capture waste events. Bluetooth and IoT sensors record environmental conditions, with alerts and analytics supporting operational review.

User experience

  • Kitchen capture app
  • Connected weighing scale
  • Temperature and humidity sensors
  • Operations dashboard
  • Waste and sustainability reporting

Delivery scope

  • Digital workflows covering kitchen audits, operating procedures, conditions, and waste
  • Dish and service-period waste analysis
  • Environmental monitoring and compliance reporting
  • Connected views for site managers and central operations

Business outcomes

Kitchen managers gained visibility into waste by dish, service period, and location. They could use these patterns to review portions, preparation, and production planning alongside environmental monitoring data.

Leadership contribution

Combined service design, hardware deployment, applied AI, and operational change. The solution had to fit the pace of a working kitchen and produce information that managers could act on each day.

India's largest honey brand

Honey Sourcing & Traceability Program

Business context

Honey passes through several participants before reaching a processing plant. The buyer needs a reliable connection between beekeeper origin, flora, harvest, transactions, and the batches received.

Fragmented records made origin verification difficult and limited visibility into sourcing patterns and procurement costs.

Objective

Establish traceability from hive to plant and give procurement and management teams a usable record of sourcing, transactions, and batch history.

My responsibility

  • Defined the product and operating workflows across beekeepers, intermediaries, vendors, and management.
  • Designed the actor-specific applications, lot and batch logic, evidence capture, and reporting requirements.
  • Balanced ease of field participation with the information needed for traceability and procurement decisions.

Operating workflow

  1. Register the beekeeper and source location, including bee-box and flora details.
  2. Create harvest lots with dates, photographs, location, and sale records.
  3. Record aggregation and trade movements using lot identities and QR scans.
  4. Create vendor batches and capture filling, moisture, purchase order, and invoice records.
  5. Record dispatch and retain the connection between vendor batches and contributing source lots.
  6. Review batch history, sourcing locations, flora-wise procurement, and participant activity.

Key decisions

  • Provide separate workflows for beekeepers, intermediaries, and vendors.
  • Support assisted lot entry for beekeepers who need help using the application.
  • Preserve source identity through aggregation and vendor batch creation.
  • Attach transaction, location, and quality fields to the operational record.
  • Give management both batch-level traceability and sourcing analysis.

Technology and controls

QR-based lot tracking connects source records, geo-tagged events, transactions, and vendor batches. Public-blockchain anchoring supports tamper-evident records, while management views and exports make the data usable for procurement.

User experience

  • Beekeeper application
  • Trader and aggregator application
  • Vendor batch and filling workflow
  • QR identification
  • Sourcing map and procurement dashboard

Delivery scope

  • Connected workflows covering all participant types from beekeeper to plant
  • Lot creation, aggregation, trade, vendor batch, and dispatch scenarios
  • Geo-tagged origin and transaction records with batch history
  • Management reporting, participant activity, and Excel/CSV exports

Business outcomes

The program connected origin evidence, procurement visibility, and traceable sourcing. Business teams could review source locations, batch history, and participant activity to support sourcing decisions and product claims.

Leadership contribution

Brought together sourcing strategy, field user experience, participant coordination, and enterprise reporting. The work addressed how an entire supply network records and uses information, including users with limited digital experience.
